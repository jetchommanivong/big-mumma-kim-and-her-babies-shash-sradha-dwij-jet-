**Decisions**

- Go with a **laser-tag game that photographs players**, with these **consent changes** from the participant chat:

### Mon 14 Sep (in chat)

### Tue 15 Sep: Lo-fi playtest (studio)

**User feedback (3 pairs)**

- It was fun to run around, and the concept was easy to grasp because laser tag is familiar.
- The suit was cumbersome, the gun too lo-fi.
- Players want **hit feedback** (vibration, sound and/or lights), a **countdown timer** rather than lives (one person suggested 60 s), and obstacles or places to hide in a small, contained space.
- People didn't mind looking bad in photos: "the sillier the better."
**Feedback from Trevor**

- **The camera should be a third party, not a participant.** The best photos capture natural reactions, not people looking at the lens.
- Design the experience first and let the photo be a by-product. Laser tag is just one way to distract people from the camera.
- Key questions: what's the experience, how is it social, and how does it use the context?
