# Interview P6

**Research project:** Why We Photograph: Meaning and Motivation Behind the Everyday Photo

---

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the last time you had your photo taken — someone else took a photo of you.

**Participant:** Last Friday, in Montville. It was my partner's birthday, we went up there, and the scenery was amazing, so I had him take photos wherever we went. I do prefer taking my own photo using a tripod though, because then you have more control.

**Interviewer:** Did you see the photos after? How did you feel?

**Participant:** I'm not a fan of how he takes my pictures, so I had to give him strict guidelines, which can get frustrating — sometimes he gets it right, sometimes not. That's why I prefer taking my own.

**Interviewer:** What's your favourite photo you've taken this year? Is there a story behind it?

**Participant:** I did a fairy-themed photoshoot of myself last week, also in Montville — the scenery was amazing, so I set up a tripod and mainly recorded myself in video mode so I could screenshot photos, plus took some regular photos too. It was a running, playing-in-the-woods, fairy-theme kind of vibe. I like them because I liked my outfit, how I posed, the angles were good, the lighting was amazing — everything just fit together well.

**Interviewer:** When you're out and about, what makes something feel worth photographing?

**Participant:** I like to take photos of everything — always have, since I was a kid, with those printable cameras with negatives. There's something I see and feel like I need to capture it. Later it became about Instagram — wanting to post what I think is nice. Then it became about filters, then graphics and editing, adding stickers or wording. Now it's more a way of life — I have to capture anything I see that's nice. Thanks to Instagram carousels, you can take photos, add strong wording, and it's a good post. So everything is worth taking a photo of, in my books.

**Interviewer:** What do you usually do with the photos you take?

**Participant:** Mainly for Instagram. I keep it on my phone, and if I lose storage, I move it to my computer.

**Interviewer:** What makes you decide to share something or keep it to yourself?

**Participant:** If it's nice, I'll share it, because I take pride in my work and want everybody to see it and think it's nice.

**Interviewer:** Who do you generally share your photos with?

**Participant:** My Instagram followers, and family group chats.

**Interviewer:** If someone scrolled through your camera roll, what impression would they get?

**Participant:** There'll be a lot of photos of myself. They'll probably think, these are nice photos — though I don't really care what other people think about my photos.

**Interviewer:** What do you think your camera roll says about you?

**Participant:** It probably shows I have ADHD, honestly — multiple genres of photos, not just myself, a view, a sunset, a nice object, anything I think is picture-worthy, even if I never use it later. They'd probably think I'm creative.

**Interviewer:** Do you think your camera roll reflects who you are as a person?

**Participant:** Oh, for sure, yes it does.

**Interviewer:** Do you care about the photos you take? Why or why not?

**Participant:** I care about them deeply. Even sitting in the same spot, every tiny movement is worth capturing, because you look different in every angle — the tiniest detail can make a difference.

**Interviewer:** Do some of the photos you take matter more than others?

**Participant:** Yes — the ones I look nice in, obviously. But I also like taking pictures of family memories, because I've lost a lot of friends and family — I don't have enough pictures of my dad, or my best friend. Since then, I try to capture every single moment. That's the main reason, I think it's heightened since I lost them.

**Interviewer:** Do you wish you'd captured more, looking back? Are there photos you tend to discard?

**Participant:** Very rarely — usually only when I run out of storage, and then I delete the ones I don't feel too sad about losing.

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me about a time someone shared a photo of you without asking?

**Participant:** No, not really — I don't recall any incident like that. I think I'm fairly photogenic, so it rarely bothers me how I look in photos. Whenever I see a camera, I pay attention and pose, so I know how to look.

**Interviewer:** Even something from a while ago?

**Participant:** I would have cared back in the day, but not anymore — it doesn't bother me, and it hasn't happened recently anyway.

**Interviewer:** Hypothetically — if someone posted a photo of you that you didn't want posted, how would you feel?

**Participant:** If it's a company posting something, since I maintain a certain public profile, I'd hate it if they posted something I didn't like — it would really annoy me, and I'd want it taken down. If it's a friend in a group chat, I wouldn't be as bothered.

**Interviewer:** Apart from asking them to take it down, would you do anything else?

**Participant:** I'd complain, write to them, make a scene about it until they take it down.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything about photography, taking photos, or being photographed that we haven't touched on that you think is important?

**Participant:** I think everybody should take more photos and pictures, and capture the special memories they'll end up having.
