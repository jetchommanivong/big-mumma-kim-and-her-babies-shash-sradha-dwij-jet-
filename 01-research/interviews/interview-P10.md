# Interview P10

**Research project:** Why We Photograph: Meaning and Motivation Behind the Everyday Photo

---

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me about the last time you had your photo taken?

**Participant:** Probably at Coachella, when we were all taking photos of each other — getting dinner for the gram, kind of thing.

**Interviewer:** How did you feel about it?

**Participant:** I was a little bit critical about it, because every time I asked for the photo and looked at it, I'd ask them to take it again, and again — I eventually gave up because I felt like I couldn't get the photo to look right.

**Interviewer:** What aspect did you dislike?

**Participant:** The proportions weren't correct, they didn't get a good angle on me. You know when someone takes a photo of you and you look at it and think, what the heck? That was probably my most recent experience with it.

**Interviewer:** Did you want real-time feedback — guidance on posing or lighting?

**Participant:** Oh yeah — eventually I just gave him the cheat code. On iPhone photography there's a portrait mode with a lot of bokeh in the background, non-photographers use it, and it gets the perfect angle, makes you look skinny, all the works.

**Interviewer:** What's the perfect angle to you?

**Participant:** A little bit up, looking down. I'm a short guy, so I don't need the low-angle boost — looking up makes my face look a bit skinnier, so I like that look.

**Interviewer:** Do you think everybody has different preferences for angles?

**Participant:** Definitely — I know some girls prefer a lower angle shot for a taller appearance, blade forward, all these camera tricks.

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me about a time somebody shared a photo of you without asking?

**Participant:** Personally, I don't mind too much if someone shares a photo of me — even a silly one, or one that's not particularly good-looking — because I enjoy the in-the-moment, raw emotion in a photo, capturing something funny, someone laughing. That's my preferred photography style. So if someone does that for me, I like it — I usually look at it, laugh, and have a good time with it. I understand people who want consent before posting, since that's their preference for their image, so I always ask before I post one of someone else.

**Interviewer:** It's good to ask before posting.

**Participant:** For me I don't mind too much, but I think if someone knows going forward that's fine — but they should ask first, as a rule.

**Interviewer:** So you're only nitpicky when you're aware and posing for it, but candid is fine?

**Participant:** Right — if it's a portrait and someone asks to take a photo, then poses me, and it looks bad, we're redoing it. But if it's just in the moment, walking and talking, chatting, experiencing something, that's fine.

**Interviewer:** Do you have a favourite photo you've taken this year?

**Participant:** I have a few. One was in Vancouver — a quick selfie moment on the way home, deliberately blurry to show the chaos and confusion of the moment, everyone not knowing what's going on. I love capturing things like that, in the moment.

Another one is of my friend — we were all taking photos by the coast during golden hour. I hate telling people to pose, because the moment you do, they stop looking natural. This one's one of my favourites because someone told him a joke and he just started laughing — it captured a perfect moment, sun and lake in the background.

**Interviewer:** Would you say you favour photos of you and your friends, portraits, because of the memories and connection attached?

**Participant:** I do like candid shots specifically because it's just come to the moment — I don't have to tell anyone to pose, it just happens. Here's one of my favourite photos of my friend, because he wasn't aware I was taking it, and the moment was just there — versus this other one, which is less candid. The reason I prefer the candid one is he was unaware in the moment, but I could still capture it for him.

**Interviewer:** When you're out and about, what makes something feel worth photographing?

**Participant:** Recently, when I see my cat doing something really cute, I want to capture that. More broadly, it's when the lighting is good. Lighting is really important to me.

**Interviewer:** What's good lighting to you?

**Participant:** If you point your phone at something and it instantly switches to night mode — that's bad lighting, you can't get a good photo. Good lighting is anything that shines on the subject, so you can take a photo at any moment without needing to boost the exposure. Or generally, golden hour — everyone loves that.

Another favourite type for me is silly, unflattering portraits of myself, just to tell a story. One of my favourites to share with friends was from after a party — me trying my best to get home, looking completely plastered, holding a pizza, looking confused. The photo tells a whole story on its own.

Overall though, besides animals and friends, my highest preference is nature photography — no need to worry about people, posing, just get the frame, get the lighting, and shoot.

**Interviewer:** Do you prefer editing your images, or keeping them raw?

**Participant:** If I really like a photo, I'll edit it a little — boost saturation, cheat a bit, make it look great.

**Interviewer:** Looking at the photos you've shared, do you feel emotions or memories attached to them?

**Participant:** Most of the nature ones came from hikes we set out to do specifically to take those photos — that was our main goal, so reaching that point at the end of the journey is what the photo represents.

**Interviewer:** What do you usually do with the photos you take? Do you share them anywhere?

**Participant:** The ones with friends — it's kind of hard to post photos of others, because I'm not sure if they'd want me to post a picture of them, so I tend to avoid it even if I enjoyed the photo a lot. You might have seen me post some from raves — those feel more okay to share.

**Interviewer:** If someone scrolled through your camera roll, what impression would they get? What do you think it says about you?

**Participant:** "This guy really likes his cat." A lot of photos of my cat, plus a lot of random, ugly selfies I take of myself to send to friends because I think it's funny.

**Interviewer:** Do you think your camera roll reflects who you are as a person?

**Participant:** Probably, yeah. You'd see me attempting aesthetic photos — I follow the rule of thirds because it's an easy guideline. Most of my photos will probably look similar, and I don't delete a lot of images, so you'll see a lot of "hey, look at this" type photos I've sent to friends.

**Interviewer:** Do you find photography is a creative outlet for you, a way to keep loved ones updated on what you're doing?

**Participant:** I like to take a photo of something so it can tell a story — like my friend with the grey sky behind him, smiling, versus him just posing for the camera.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything you'd like to add about photography, taking photos, or being photographed?

**Participant:** Something important — even at a convention, the big rule is to ask before doing anything. It's important because not everyone enjoys having their photo taken, and if you do take one, you should show them. Privacy and consent are very important — not everyone wants to be seen on public forums or online. Just because I don't mind, doesn't mean someone else wouldn't.
