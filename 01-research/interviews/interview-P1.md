# Interview P1

**Research project:** Why We Photograph: Meaning and Motivation Behind the Everyday Photo
**Date:** 21 August 2026

---

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me about the last time you had your photo taken, in a non-professional context?

**Participant:** Yesterday, with my family. I usually go on a run with my wife, and we take a selfie at some point.

**Interviewer:** Is that to celebrate the run?

**Participant:** Yeah, it's a bit of a milestone — reaching a certain point. We take a selfie and a group pic together, then go on our way. Sometimes it's also about accountability, showing we've done a certain task.

**Interviewer:** What's your favourite photo you've taken this year? Is there a story behind it?

**Participant:** We went on an overseas trip. I think it was just the series of documentary-style, photojournalistic photographs I love taking. It took me back to the joy of photography.

**Interviewer:** When you're out and about, what makes something feel worth photographing to you?

**Participant:** Something that tells a story, and has a little bit of juxtaposition — something that doesn't belong there and just sticks out. I love that kind of contrast, like graffiti art in a more bohemian setting, but in an urban part of the city. Cultures clashing. It's like capturing timeless moments in an urban sprawl.

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me what you usually do with your photos? Do you share them, or is it a hobby?

**Participant:** If it's worthy of sharing, which isn't often, I share it — and I'm proud of it when I do. Early in my career I used to upload to Flickr, using only my professional/travel cameras, not my phone. I sometimes include the EXIF data — focal length, aperture, shutter speed — just to document my skills.

**Interviewer:** Do you post on a personal page, or a professional one?

**Participant:** I have a photography business account on Instagram, which is a little soulless because I only upload photographs I deem worthy — rather than everyday snaps of me and my family. I feel like maybe I should upload those too, since that's the beauty of having a phone camera — the best camera is the one you always have. Maybe I should stop being so purist and use my phone more for the hobby.

**Interviewer:** If someone scrolled through your camera roll, what impression would they get? What do you think it says about you?

**Participant:** I love running and cycling, and taking photographs of my work — I do a lot of workshop facilitation, so a lot of photos are of students working, sticky notes, outputs. Could be pretty meaningless to someone else. The faces that are consistent and familiar would be myself and my family.

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me about a time someone shared a photo of you without asking?

**Participant:** That happens, and I'm mindful of moments where I know my photo will be taken — but because it's a public event, like a work event, I haven't explicitly consented, though by attending I think it's taken as consent. I know I'm being documented at big conference-like events. You're always aware someone's documenting you, out of the corner of your eye. It makes you watch yourself, watch what you're doing.

**Interviewer:** Before we finish, is there anything you'd like to add?

**Participant:** As a photographer, I think I should take more photos for fun and not just see it as work. Every time I pick up my good cameras and lenses, it's usually for work — I should make an effort to use it for fun, documenting family life too.
