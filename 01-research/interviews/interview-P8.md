# Interview P8

**Research project:** Why We Photograph: Meaning and Motivation Behind the Everyday Photo

*Note: merged from two recording segments — recording was paused and resumed partway through.*

---

**Interviewer:** Do I have your consent to record?

**Participant:** Yes.

**Interviewer:** Hi, thanks so much for taking the time to talk with me today. The project we're exploring focuses on how people relate to photography — including the photos we take, the photos taken of us, and what they mean to us. This is a casual conversation, and there are no right or wrong answers. Feel free to take your time, tell stories, and go on tangents. If you don't want to answer a particular question, we can move on.

Can you tell me about the last time you had your photo taken? What was the occasion, or who took the photo?

**Participant:** Does that also count as taking one on the phone?

**Interviewer:** Yeah, phone, camera, anything.

**Participant:** I think it was just a selfie I took to tell my friend how tired I was.

**Interviewer:** Oh really? How did you feel about it?

**Participant:** It's really good, because you can just see my face, how tired I am, all that stuff, very straightforwardly. It expresses the feeling as well. So, really tired.

**Interviewer:** Why were you tired?

**Participant:** Because I'd had a full day that day.

**Interviewer:** Oh, I see, so you'd worked a full day shift and you were tired.

**Participant:** Yeah.

**Interviewer:** Should we stop?

*[Recording paused here and resumed for the second segment.]*

**Interviewer:** What's your favourite photo you've taken this year? Tell me the story behind it.

**Participant:** I usually take photos for church events. I think I took a really good photo once when someone was worshipping. I liked it because I'd never seen that person look this good in real life. That's, for me, down to my photography skill. I think that's why I like it.

**Interviewer:** Do you go to church as well? Is that how you got the photo — do you take photos at church often?

**Participant:** I'd say church is more like a job for me, a volunteer job. I actually prefer taking street photos, like taking photos of people on the street and stuff. I like that more.

**Interviewer:** When you're out, what makes something feel like you want to take a photo of it?

**Participant:** I think it's probably people. I like photographing people who work at markets, or really traditional places.

**Interviewer:** So do you like taking photos of them candidly, without them noticing?

**Participant:** Yeah, I prefer that to having them look at the camera and pose, because if they look at the camera, I kind of need to give them instructions, tell them what pose to do, and I'm really bad at that. So I prefer it when they don't notice. That's why I love using a long lens — you can capture it from a distance without them noticing.

**Interviewer:** What do you usually do with the photos you've taken? Do you post them on social media, save them and forget about them, or print them out?

**Participant:** If I like the photo, I'll post it on Instagram. I've got an account just for posting photos, not many people follow it, but that's just how I use it.

**Interviewer:** Is it a public account?

**Participant:** Yeah, it's public. I feel like a lot of photographers have photography accounts they just want to post to — it's kind of like a public Google Drive to show what photos they take. Sometimes a really famous photographer would like your photo, like, "oh, is this photo good?"

**Interviewer:** Has that happened to you before?

**Participant:** Yeah, I took a photo in Korea, of street cats, and there was a guy — I don't know who he was, but he had the blue tick.

**Interviewer:** A blue tick? How many followers does he have?

**Participant:** I'm not really sure, but I think he's a famous photographer with black-and-white photos. This was many years ago, though.

**Interviewer:** If someone scrolled through your camera roll, what impression would they get of you? What do you think it says about you as a person?

**Participant:** I think it depends on the device. On my phone it would be really random — I save a lot of screenshots. I think I have a bit of ADHD, so I'll screenshot a lot of things thinking, "okay, I'm going to do this later," and then never actually do it. So they'd probably think this person is easily forgetful.

**Interviewer:** Do you care about the photos you take, whether it's on your phone or camera?

**Participant:** If it's on my phone, I don't really care about it. But if it's on a camera, especially a film camera, I care about it a lot, because you have to spend money on it — you only get one shot. So it depends on the device.

**Interviewer:** Lastly, can you tell me about a time someone shared a photo of you without asking? Maybe they posted a photo on Instagram that you were in, and didn't ask you first.

**Participant:** I think probably at church — they'd just post photos for events without asking.

**Interviewer:** Do you usually care about that?

**Participant:** It depends on what I look like. If I look really bad, but no, I don't really care.
